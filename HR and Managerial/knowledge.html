1. Your 60-second answer to “What do you know about MathWorks?”

MathWorks is the company behind MATLAB and Simulink. It was founded in 1984 by Jack Little and Cleve Moler, is based in Natick, Massachusetts, is privately held, and has more than 6,500 people. Engineers and scientists use the tools in areas like automotive, aerospace, medical devices and communications; the website's customer stories include vehicle software and an experiment that makes oxygen on Mars.

The guiding principle is “do the right thing”, and the core values are continuous improvement, respect and invest, a rational workplace, learning and self-improvement, and credibility and integrity.

In India the team is MathWorks' largest outside the US, with offices in Bangalore, Hyderabad, New Delhi and Pune. EDG is the program where new engineers explore teams, get mentoring and customer exposure, and then settle into the team that fits them.

How to deliver it

Say it in under a minute, then stop and add one line about you: “What attracts me most is that the users are engineers and scientists, because I like building for people who depend on the tool.” Don't read it like a list. Say the facts in your own words.

3. Products and customers: just enough, honestly

  • MATLAB: matrices and numerical computing, machine learning and deep learning, image and signal processing, predictive maintenance. The name comes from “matrix laboratory” (general knowledge; Cleve Moler wrote the first version).
  • Simulink: you build a system as block diagrams and simulate it (wireless, electrification, control systems, autonomous systems, robotics, AI). Model-Based Design = design the system as a model, simulate and verify it, then generate code from it.
  • The users are engineers and scientists. A bug in MathWorks software can end up inside someone's car or medical device, so correctness and quality matter a lot. That one idea explains the values on the next section (Credibility, Excellence).
  • A staff quote on the About Us page (a senior software engineer): “Every design decision we make affects at least a subset of MATLAB users.” Great line to remember for “what excites you about the work”.
Be honest about MATLAB

Your resume does not list MATLAB (the JD says experience is “a plus”, and the EDG page says “You don't have to be a MATLAB expert.”). Don't pretend. If you get time, do the free MATLAB Onramp course (MathWorks itself suggests it on its interviewing page). It takes about 2 hours and lets you say “I completed MATLAB Onramp this week and tried …” instead of “no”.

4. Mission and the 5 core values (exact headings)

Mission: “Our purpose is to change the world by accelerating the pace of discovery, innovation, development, and learning” in engineering and science. Guiding principle: “do the right thing”, defined on the values page as doing what is best for staff, customers, business partners and communities for the long term. The mission has four pillars: Technology, Business, Human, Social.

Never recite them. Tell the story first, then name the value once, in one short line. 5. EDG: what it really is

  • Official wording: “EDG is a leadership development program within the MathWorks Development organization” (job posting). The India page says it “focuses on technical coaching and leadership development for recent graduates”. (The US/UK pages say “Group”, India says “Program”. Both are the same thing.)
  • Not one fixed project: you select and explore collaborative technical projects, find the teams that match your interests, and then move into one. Managers coach you on leadership.
  • Three parts: tailored technical training, a personalised Customer Experience Curriculum, and project work with technical teams of your choice. Plus mentoring and knowledge-sharing forums.
  • Top 3 reasons on the EDG page: Flexibility (explore different teams), Continuous Learning, Collaboration. A staff quote: “rarely are two days the same”.
  • Responsibilities in the job posting: contribute code or product demos; build customer-perspective and problem-solving skills on complex MATLAB and Simulink issues; use agentic-AI-based tools to support your work; show initiative, lead process-improvement projects, coach and mentor others.
  • Who joins: the interviewing page says the EDG team is about half engineers and half computer scientists, and most EDGers join development teams, so software development skills are key.
  • Not on any page I read: how long the rotation lasts, or an exact list of EDG teams. If asked, say you'd like to understand that and ask them (that is a good question to ask).

7. Bonus: the origin story (impresses in 20 seconds)

  • Cleve Moler wrote the first version of MATLAB. He was a maths and computer-science professor, and an author of the LINPACK and EISPACK numerical libraries. The site lists him as chief mathematician and cofounder (1939–2026).
  • Jack Little (CEO, MIT and Stanford engineering degrees) was a coauthor and principal architect of early MATLAB and the Signal Processing and Control System toolboxes. They founded MathWorks in 1984.
  • Line to use: “MATLAB began as a way for engineers to use matrix libraries without writing Fortran or C, and that idea of making hard computation easy for the user is what I like about the product.”
